## When you might want a commercial license

A commercial license is appropriate if you want to:

- **Embed CaptureSpike in a proprietary product** without
  the obligation to release your source code under GPL-3.0
- **Distribute CaptureSpike internally at a company** that
  does not want to comply with GPL-3.0's source-availability
  requirements
- **Add proprietary modifications** that you do not want to
  share with the community
- **Receive indemnification or a warranty** (the GPL-3.0
  explicitly disclaims both)

If you only want to use CaptureSpike internally, modify it
for your own use, or contribute improvements back to the
project, the GPL-3.0 license is sufficient and free.

## Pricing

Commercial licenses are sold on a per-organization,
per-use-case basis. Pricing depends on the number of
developers, the number of end users, and the scope of
the modifications. As a rough guide:

- **Small business / indie embedder:** $1,000 / year
- **Mid-size product team:** $5,000 / year
- **Enterprise (redistributed to > 10,000 users):**
  custom pricing

A perpetual (one-time) license is also available at 5x
the annual price.

## Contact

To inquire about a commercial license, **open a GitHub
Issue** at https://github.com/Interfer0/CaptureSpike/issues/new

- Title must start with `[commercial]`
- Body should include:
  - Your company name and website
  - A short description of the product or use case
  - Approximate number of developers and end users
  - Whether you need modifications from upstream

GitHub does not have a direct-message feature, so issues
are the only built-in way to reach the project owner
without exposing a personal email address. Issues with
`[commercial]` in the title are filtered and replied to
privately via the issue thread (you'll be asked to share
a contact email in the reply).

We typically respond within 1-2 business days.

## What you get

A commercial license includes:

- A signed license agreement tailored to your use case
- Permission to ship CaptureSpike (and any modifications)
  under your own proprietary terms
- A copy of the source code (same as what's on GitHub)
- Optional: priority email support and bug-fix SLAs
  (separate agreement)

The commercial license does **not** include access to
private development branches, roadmap influence, or
co-marketing unless separately agreed.

## What stays open source

The GPL-3.0 version on GitHub remains free and open
source. Commercial customers do not get to "lock" the
code; if they stop paying, the latest GPL-3.0 release
remains available to everyone.
